SAMPLE REPORT 01 · IT SUPPORT

IT Health Check Report

Example client: Mokoena Office Services (fictional) · Sample scope: 8 workstations, 1 router and basic user/device practices.

Report IDDEMO-ITH-001
Sample review date28 October 2026
Assessment typeBasic IT health check
Overall example statusNeeds attention
Priority 12Urgent actions
Priority 23Near-term actions
Priority 32Improvements

Executive summary

This fictional example shows a small office with inconsistent update evidence, no recently verified restore test and an incomplete device inventory. These conditions can increase downtime and make recovery harder. Findings should be confirmed against the real environment before decisions are made.

Example findings

FindingPriorityBusiness impactRecommended action
Backup restore test not evidencedHighFiles may not be recoverable when neededConfirm backup coverage and perform an authorised test restore
Updates not consistently documentedHighKnown issues may remain unpatchedReview update status and establish a recurring patch check
Device inventory incompleteMediumAssets and ownership may be missedRecord device, user, operating system and support status
Local admin access not reviewedMediumExcess privileges can increase impact of compromiseReview administrator accounts and remove unnecessary access with approval

Prioritised action plan

Within 7 days: verify backup coverage and arrange a safe restore test; confirm security update status.
Within 30 days: complete the asset register and review local administrator access.
Within 60 days: set a recurring maintenance checklist and assign action owners.

Scope and limitations

A basic health check is a point-in-time review of agreed devices and practices. It does not automatically include penetration testing, forensic examination, full network discovery, software licensing validation or a guarantee that no vulnerabilities exist.

Cybersecurity Assessment Report

Example client: Khanyisa Trading (fictional) · Sample scope: identity/access practices, endpoint protection evidence, backups and incident readiness.

Report IDDEMO-CYB-001
Sample review date28 October 2026
Assessment typeScoped security review
Overall example statusElevated risk
High3Example findings

Executive summary

This fictional sample illustrates how a scoped review can identify gaps in multi-factor authentication (MFA) coverage, privileged access review and incident preparation. Severity labels are illustrative; actual risk ratings require evidence, likelihood and business-impact context.

Example risk register

Risk / observationSeverityPotential impactRecommended treatment
MFA coverage not confirmed for all usersHighAccount takeover and unauthorised accessVerify coverage and enforce MFA using an approved rollout plan
Privileged accounts lack a documented reviewHighExcessive access or delayed removal of accessInventory admin accounts and approve least-privilege changes
Incident response contacts and steps are unclearHighSlower containment and recovery during an incidentDocument escalation contacts, evidence handling and response roles
Backup recovery evidence is incompleteMediumExtended interruption or data lossDefine recovery priorities and test restoration
Security awareness refresh is overdueMediumGreater exposure to phishing and social engineeringRun short role-relevant awareness sessions and report suspicious messages
Policy review dates are not recordedLowGuidance may become outdatedAssign an owner and annual review schedule

Recommended next steps

Immediate: confirm MFA status and privileged access with the system owner; do not make changes without authorisation.
Within 30 days: document incident response steps, owners and escalation contacts.
Within 60 days: test backup recovery, deliver targeted awareness and track remediation evidence.

Assessment method and limitations

A cybersecurity assessment is limited to the agreed scope, access and evidence available. This sample does not claim that live systems were scanned or tested. Vulnerability testing is only performed with explicit written authorisation and an agreed test boundary. This is not an ISO 27001 certification or legal compliance opinion.
